What changed on this site, and when. Substantive corrections and data updates are listed; typographic fixes are not.

Why this page exists. Every page here carries a "last updated" date, and this is what sits behind it. EU climate legislation moves monthly: a figure that was right in June can be superseded by an implementing regulation in August and a Parliament vote in September. If you cited something from this site, this page tells you whether the thing you cited has since changed. Where a change corrects something that was wrong rather than merely out of date, it says so.
29 October 2026

The energy map’s live files, caught up to the end of October

  • Energy mapBoth halves of the Grids Package are in trilogue, which the map did not say. It recorded the Council’s general approach of 26 June 2026 and stopped there, leaving out Parliament entirely. The permitting directive: Industry Committee report 2 July 2026 (A10-0195/2026, rapporteur Niels Fuglsang, S&D), mandate confirmed in plenary 8 July, second trilogue in Strasbourg on 6 October. The TEN-E recast: committee report 10 September (A10-0231/2026, rapporteur Tsvetelina Penkova, S&D), mandate confirmed 16 September, second trilogue 13 October, at which mandatory sensitivity analyses in network planning were settled while the delegated-versus- implementing-act question and the sequencing of scenarios were held over. Both files reconvene on 17 November and the presidency wants them agreed before the end of the year.
  • Energy mapThe network charges proposal has met resistance in Council. COM(2026) 600 was on the map as tabled and nothing more. The Irish presidency circulated compromise texts on 29 September and 2 October; member states have challenged the harmonisation of national methodologies through Commission guidelines, the legal basis of the taxation element and the naming of particular user categories for preferential tariffs. Ministers debated it on 29 October, a third compromise was due on 10 November, and the harmonisation provision may not survive it.
  • Energy mapFour acts now say how badly they are being implemented. From the Commission’s October 2026 infringement package: nine member states under reasoned opinion for missing building renovation plans under the recast buildings directive; four for incomplete transposition of the recast efficiency directive; Croatia for the Renewable Energy Directive; and Bulgaria referred to the Court of Justice over the 2019 electricity directive, with financial sanctions requested. A map of what the law says is worth little without some sense of where it is not being followed.
  • Energy mapA note on what is coming but not yet tabled. The energy security package — a revision of the gas, oil and electricity security of supply framework, with an EU-level electricity alarm and mandated demand reduction in the reported drafts — is due this quarter. The post-2030 energy framework, and with it the question of whether there is a renewables target after 2030 or a technology-neutral clean energy one, may slip to 2027. Neither is on the map, because the map holds acts and proposals rather than intentions, so both are described in “About this map” instead.
28 October 2026

The European Climate Resilience Law joins the map

  • Legislation mapCOM(2026) 730, tabled 27 October 2026. A proposed regulation under Article 192(1) TFEU, procedure 2026/0336(COD), and the first attempt to put adaptation on the same legal footing as mitigation. Three things in it bind: a reference global warming trajectory — 1.5°C by 2030, 2°C by 2050, 3°C by 2100 — as the common planning assumption; regular national climate risk assessments, the EEA’s by 30 June 2028 and national ones by 30 June 2029; and a single national strategic plan on climate resilience by 31 December 2030 in place of four existing planning instruments. For every major risk in its Annex II a member state must name the public authority that owns it. Reporting moves to one biennial cycle from 15 March 2029, which is what its Article 14 amendment to the Governance Regulation does.
  • Legislation mapIt went into the lane that was already there. The map has had an adaptation and resilience lane since it was built, holding the Floods Directive, the Civil Protection Mechanism and its 2021 amendment, and the Critical Entities Resilience Directive — so the new act joins those four rather than starting a category. It links to the Governance Regulation as an amendment and to the Climate Law, the Floods Directive, the Critical Entities Directive, the Civil Protection Mechanism and the Nature Restoration Regulation as related acts. The map now holds 109 acts, 38 of them at core level.
  • Energy mapOne line on the other map. The Climate Resilience Law amends the Governance Regulation, which is one of the twenty acts that appear on both maps, so the energy map’s entry for it now says what would change: Article 19 reporting, and the adaptation elements of the national energy and climate plans moving into the new resilience plan. Its stamp now distinguishes the date its act data was checked, 27 September, from this single later addition, rather than implying the whole map was reviewed today.
  • Legislation mapA stamp that had been wrong for three weeks. The toolbar carried its own short date, “Updated 5 Oct 2026”, separate from the one in the footer, and it was missed by the updates of 15, 20 and 26 October. Both now say the same thing.
  • Legislation mapThe note on adaptation in “About this map” has a paragraph saying what the proposal would gather up, since that note previously described the scattered architecture the new law is a response to. The accompanying Communication, COM(2026) 720, is named in the act’s summary but is not on the map: it is a communication, and the map holds binding law and proposals for it.
26 October 2026

A deal on invalidation, Parliament’s position adopted, and a summit the last two updates missed

  • EU ETSThe invalidation question is settled, at 700 Mt. Council and Parliament reached a provisional agreement on 20 October 2026, announced the next day. Invalidation survives — the Commission had proposed abolishing it — and the threshold rises from 400 to 700 million allowances as soon as the act is formally adopted, between Parliament’s 650 Mt and the Council’s 800 Mt from 2031. Whether the threshold should come down again is left to the wider ETS review. The three-position table on the ETS page gains a fourth row for the outcome; the deal still needs endorsement and formal adoption, so it is not law yet.
  • BenchmarksParliament adopted its position on COM(2026) 619 without changing it. The plenary took over the committee text on 22 October 2026 by 474 votes to 82 with 28 abstentions (A10-0276/2026, adopted text T10-0363/2026) and sent the file straight back to committee for negotiation with the Council. The panel now describes a first-reading position rather than a committee one.
  • BenchmarksParliament’s position is in the chart too, as a derived value. Parliament publishes no pair to set beside the Council’s 43.0 and 38.7, so the fourth bar applies the reported 12.5% cap to the 2021–2025 benchmarks, giving 41.4 for heat and 37.3 for fuel. That is this page’s arithmetic on a reported figure, not a published value; the legend, the tooltip, the text and a comment in the source all say so. The three headline cards now name the Council as the source of 43.0, 38.7 and the +38%, which they did not before.
  • ETS 2The European Council of 15–16 October was missed by both the 15 and the 20 October updates. Poland, Czechia, Slovakia and Hungary, supported by Italy and Greece, pressed to postpone ETS 2 to 2030 or later and did not get it. The conclusions leave the 2028 date alone and instead ask the Commission to "update the assessment of all impacts of ETS 2, in particular its design and safeguards" (paragraph 8), while calling on the co-legislators to accelerate the market stability reserve file (paragraph 7) — which they did, five days later. The ETS 2 page said nothing about any of it.
  • CBAMThe downstream extension reached formal trilogue. Technical preparation began on 28 September and the first formal trilogue was held on 20 October 2026, with a second provisionally set for 30 November and political agreement targeted before the end of the year. The page said only that trilogues had been under way since late September.
  • SiteThe energy legislation map has been added, as a companion rather than a page. It covers the ground the climate map leaves out on purpose: 147 acts in eight lanes, from the 1990 electricity transit directive to the July 2026 proposals, with a renewables control that rings every act carrying renewable energy provisions, and 20 acts that appear on both maps and link across. It lives at energy.html and keeps its own header and layout, because it is a different subject rather than another chapter of this one. Its data stamp stays at 27 September 2026, which is when its content was last checked — only its home has changed.
  • SiteA link in the top right of every page, shaped unlike anything else in the bar. A rounded, teal-tinted pill carrying the map’s own icon, pushed to the right-hand end of the navigation, so that following it reads as leaving the climate explorer rather than moving within it. The home page carries the same thing as a separate card below the Explore grid.
  • SiteA new icon for it. The energy map had inherited the site favicon, the EU flag with “EU ETS” across it, which said the wrong thing twice over. It now has its own: the same flag and ring of twelve stars, with a stylised solar panel standing in front of them. Drawn as a PNG favicon and as an SVG for the navigation pill.
  • EU ETSThe allowance price series runs to September 2026, and the gap since June is closed. Three months were added — €80.26 for July, €81.63 for August, €84.62 for September — from EEX’s own primary market auction report for 2026, the market operator’s record of every auction and its clearing price. It is not the splice this page spent three updates refusing to make: rebuilding the first half of 2026 from the EEX file returns the six monthly means already plotted, to the cent, from the same 107 observations and with the same half-year mean of €75.39. ICAP’s primary-market series for the EU ETS is the auction series, so there is no seam at June. The annual point for 2026 is now €77.80 over 166 observations to 30 September, the chart header reads 3 084 observations, and the note explaining why the line stopped has been replaced by one explaining why it no longer does.
  • EU ETSOctober is deliberately not plotted, because the month is not finished. Its 17 auctions to 26 October average €85.43, which is now the fifth headline figure under the chart, and the auction of 26 October cleared at €87.68, the highest since mid-January.
  • CBAMThe Q3 certificate price now has an arithmetic check behind it. Volume-weighting the 59 auctions of July to September from the same EEX file reproduces the Commission’s published €82.32 exactly — the simple mean of those auctions is €82.18, and the difference is the volume weighting. Recorded in the sources on both pages.
  • CBAMWhat the UK will actually charge. HMRC published the first illustrative UK CBAM rates on 16 October 2026 — £9.69 a tonne of CO₂e for iron and steel, £9.79 for aluminium, £9.24 for hydrogen, £8.78 for cement, £1.45 for fertilisers — alongside draft default values. A new block on the countries tab sets out why those look a tenth of the EU figure and are not comparable to it: the UK nets each sector’s remaining free allocation out of the rate itself, where the EU charges the full allowance price and handles free allocation on its own side of the border through the CBAM factor. Nothing here is binding; the real rates for the first quarter of 2027 are due before the end of this year.
  • CBAMThe page said nothing about verification, which is a hole in a compliance page. A declaration for 2026 imports must rest on verified emissions, and the verifier profession is being built from nothing: the first accreditation anywhere went to EmiCert in Greece in September 2026, Accredia has named three Italian bodies and Swedac accredited Intertek on 13 October. Five is what can be documented from national registers; the Commission’s own list had not been published. A new panel on the compliance tab explains why the count matters, given one nine-month declaration window ending 30 September 2027, a site visit required at first verification, and accreditation granted per activity group.
  • CBAMThe downstream trilogue ended without agreement. Today’s earlier entry recorded that the first formal trilogue was held on 20 October; the readout is that both sides set out their mandates and no deal followed, with four questions open — the 50-tonne threshold for light finished aluminium goods, pre-consumer scrap as a precursor, online platforms, and default values for the added goods. Next round 30 November.
  • ETS 2The reserve is not something being added — it has been there since 2023. The page described the €45 price trigger and this month’s strengthening act without ever saying that ETS 2 has its own market stability reserve, separate from the ETS 1 one, set up by the same 2023 directive with 600 million allowances in it from the start, and emptied or filled by volume triggers at 440 and 210 million allowances in circulation as well as by price. Added, so that the recent amendment reads as what it is: a change to existing machinery.
  • SiteChecked, nothing to change. The Q4 2026 CBAM certificate price is not due until 4 January 2027, which is what the price table already says; a figure of €84.76 circulating this month is a third party’s running average of the Q4 auctions so far, not an official price, and has not been put on the page. The ETS 2 reserve amendment adopted on 9 October has not yet appeared in the Official Journal. The resumed IMO session is still 4 December.

  • BenchmarksThere are now three positions on COM(2026) 619, not two. 4 October 2026

The fall-back benchmark proposal, charted

  • BenchmarksNew section on the overview tab: when a benchmark is not a product benchmark. The page said the two fall-backs are "used where no product benchmark fits" without saying when that happens. It now sets out the cascade — product benchmark, then heat, then fuel, then process emissions, in the strict order of priority under Article 10(2) of the Free Allocation Regulation — and the fact that allocation is calculated per sub-installation rather than per installation, which is what makes the cascade work. With the cases you actually meet the fall-backs in: outputs with no product benchmark, parts of a benchmarked plant outside its system boundary, heat exported to district heating, and direct firing where no metered heat exists.
  • BenchmarksThe same section records where the two fall-back values came from, which explains why the statutory clamp hits them harder than anything else. Unlike the other 52 they were never measured best practice: the Commission derived them from literature, "based on the use of natural gas as reference fuel and a boiler efficiency of 90% for the heat benchmark". The 2013–2020 fuel benchmark of 56.1 allowances per TJ is exactly the emission factor of natural gas, and 56.1 ÷ 0.9 gives the heat benchmark of 62.3. Applying a best-practice reduction rate to a gas-boiler reference is how the 2026–2030 values end up below what gas-fired heat can achieve.
  • BenchmarksCOM(2026) 619 now appears as a scenario on the reduction rates tab, not just as a sentence. The heat and fuel fall-backs are the clearest case of the Article 10a(2) clamp doing the work rather than the data — both sit at the maximum 50% total reduction, which is why they fell about a third in one step. The proposal would lower that maximum for these two values alone. The panel charts the 2021–2025 value, the value in force and the indicative value under the proposal, side by side.
  • BenchmarksThe indicative figures — 43.0 and 38.7 allowances per TJ, about 38% above the values in force — are from recital 2 of the Council's negotiating mandate of 16 September 2026, which gives them as indicative averages for 2026–2030. The proposal's own text fixes no percentage at all: it sets the rate at whatever "makes full use of the additional amount" of roughly 80 million allowances without triggering the cross-sectoral correction factor. The panel says so, and is drawn as an open bar rather than a solid one.
  • BenchmarksTwo features of the proposal that are easy to miss are set out alongside: oil and gas activities (NACE 0610, 0620, 1920 and 4950) are excluded and stay on the rate in force, so the fall-backs would become two values rather than one; and the adjusted values run from 2027, with the 2026 top-up paid together with the 2027 allocation at a rate using 40% of the adjustment. The scenario is therefore shown as a period level, not a year-by-year path.
  • BenchmarksThe full table, the calculator and the derivation stay on the law in force. Nothing in the scenario feeds them.
  • FixOpening the benchmarks page at a tab threw an error. benchmarks.html#rates, #cbam and #calc have been failing on first paint since deep links were added on 21 September: an older hash handler near the top of the page switched tabs before the chart variables further down had been initialised. That handler is gone — the shared deep-link script at the end of the page, which runs after everything is ready, was already doing the same job.
3 October 2026

Procedural status across the pending files, and a correction on the CRCF

  • CorrectionRegulation (EU) 2024/3012 was under the wrong name. The map called it the Carbon Removals Certification Framework, after its subject matter. The Commission's own name for it is the Carbon Removals and Carbon Farming (CRCF) Regulation, and that is what it is now called here, with the older name noted so a search for it still lands.
  • EU ETSAll three institutions now have a position on the MSR invalidation file, and no two agree. The Council adopted its negotiating mandate on 23 September 2026: suspend invalidation until 31 December 2030, then reinstate it at 800 Mt from 1 January 2031. That sits against Parliament's 650 Mt of 15 September and the Commission's proposal to abolish it. The three are now set out side by side, with trilogues under way and agreement targeted before the end of 2026.
  • Legislation mapProcedure numbers and committee stage added to the pending proposals. The ETS review is 2026/0212(COD), Environment Committee lead, rapporteur Peter Liese, indicative plenary 14 December 2026. The maritime MRV simplification is 2026/0210(COD) under the same rapporteur and the same indicative date. The MSR amendment is 2026/0085(COD), rapporteur Pierfrancesco Maran.
  • BenchmarksThe fall-back benchmark uplift is on the urgent track. COM(2026) 619 is procedure 2026/0211(COD) with Adam Jarubas (EPP) as rapporteur. The Environment Committee requested the urgent procedure on 4 September 2026, the Economic and Social Committee gave its opinion on 24 September, and a plenary first reading is pencilled in for 19 October — well ahead of the review it was tabled alongside, which makes it the change most likely to reach the heat and fuel values first.
  • Legislation mapImplementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2358 added — the first operating layer under the CRCF, adopted 20 November 2025. It governs how certification schemes are recognised, how certification bodies are appointed and supervised, how audits run and what the registries must do to stop a removal being counted twice. The methodologies for the activities themselves are still to come as delegated regulations, so the framework can currently accredit an auditor but not yet certify a tonne. It sits at machinery level, which takes the map to 108 acts: 37 core, 79 with machinery.
  • CBAMThe downstream extension has been in trilogue since late September, rather than merely awaiting one.
  • EU ETSThe ETS 2 market stability reserve amendment now records the provisional agreement of 11 June 2026 — the release above the €45 trigger doubled to 40 million allowances, the reserve continuing beyond 2030, and a gradual release below 260 million in circulation — and that it is awaiting formal adoption.

Also flagged that the adjourned IMO extraordinary session reconvenes in October 2026.

  • SiteNarrow screens. Every page was wider than a phone viewport — up to twice the width on the ETS, CBAM, benchmarks and legislation pages — forcing horizontal scrolling and clipping content. Fixed: the header link row wraps, two-column blocks collapse to one, the tab strips scroll horizontally, and the legislation map's detail panel no longer extends the document.
  • SiteTabs are now linkable. The legislation map exports all 107 acts with their number, title, year, policy area, status, EUR-Lex link and relations.   • AccessibilityCharts and tabs are now reachable without sight or a mouse. Every chart canvas carries a name and points at the CSV as its text alternative, rather than being an unlabelled blank to a screen reader. The tab strips implement the ARIA tab pattern — tablist, tab, tabpanel, aria-selected — and respond to left, right, home and end keys. Focus outlines are visible again. The legislation map, which is drawn on a canvas and cannot be made readable in place, carries a description pointing at the CSV and the search field.
