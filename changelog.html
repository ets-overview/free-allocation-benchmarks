What changed on this site, and when. Substantive corrections and data updates are listed; typographic fixes are not.

Why this page exists. Every page here carries a "last updated" date, and this is what sits behind it. EU climate legislation moves monthly: a figure that was right in June can be superseded by an implementing regulation in August and a Parliament vote in September. If you cited something from this site, this page tells you whether the thing you cited has since changed. Where a change corrects something that was wrong rather than merely out of date, it says so.
21 September 2026

September policy update, a new ETS 2 page, and a navigation rebuild

  • EU ETSMarket Stability Reserve. Added Parliament's position on COM(2026) 153. The Environment Committee rejected the Commission's approach on 10 September and the plenary adopted its negotiating position on 15 September, 367–240 with 59 abstentions: keep invalidation, but raise the threshold from 400 Mt to 650 Mt. Council remains divided; trilogues follow. The page previously said only that the proposal was not law.
  • EU ETSThe rest of the July 2026 review. New panel covering what COM(2026) 616 contains besides the cap trajectory: free allocation made conditional on verified decarbonisation investment plans from 2031, 250 Mt of permanent removals integrated, international credits from 2036, municipal waste incineration from 2031, and the Industrial Decarbonisation Bank.
  • New pageEU ETS 2 now has its own page. It was a box on the ETS scope tab. At 957 Mt reported for 2024 it is larger than aviation and maritime combined and starts pricing in 2028, so it now gets the same treatment as the other sectors: scope, the obligation on fuel suppliers, the cap, the €45 price safeguard, the Social Climate Fund and the overlap with effort sharing. The country breakdown chart moved there from the ETS page.
  • CBAMDefault values corrected. Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1740 of 31 July 2026 corrects the Annex I default values with retroactive effect from 1 January 2026, affecting selected aluminium products and precursors of unknown origin. The calculator and the default value explorer still use the 4 February 2026 version; both now say so explicitly. The corrected values have not yet been transcribed.
  • CBAMDownstream extension has moved. Council general approach of 12 June 2026 (around 200 goods, annual review from 2028) and Parliament's negotiating position of 15 September 2026, adopted 464–50, pushing towards 457 products. The page previously described this only as a December 2025 proposal.
  • CorrectionCOM(2026) 619 was the wrong citation. The benchmarks page attributed the reintroduction of 15% of phased-out free allocation and the 2038 CBAM phase-out to COM(2026) 619. That change is in COM(2026) 616, the ETS review; COM(2026) 619 is the separate fall-back benchmark uplift for the heat and fuel benchmarks. Both pages now say which is which.
  • AviationThe CORSIA question has been answered. The page said the Commission's assessment of CORSIA was unresolved. It was published in July 2026 and was negative. COM(2026) 616 proposes extending the ETS from 2029 to departing flights within 5 000 km of Frankfurt, with a deduction for CORSIA costs and a further review in 2032. Added to the scope and CORSIA tabs; the caveat saying the proposal was out of scope has been removed.
  • MaritimeJuly 2026 proposals added. Ships of 400–5 000 GT would enter the pricing obligation from 2031 with monitoring from 2029; the ice-class and outermost region derogations would run to 2035, as would a new derogation for containerships of 10 000 TEU and above on voyages over 300 nautical miles. Also flagged that the adjourned IMO extraordinary session reconvenes in October 2026.
  • SiteNavigation. The benchmarks, maritime, aviation and new ETS 2 pages were reachable only from the home page and from cross-links. The EU ETS item in the top navigation is now a dropdown listing all five, and the navigation is identical on every page.
  • SiteNarrow screens. Every page was wider than a phone viewport — up to twice the width on the ETS, CBAM, benchmarks and legislation pages — forcing horizontal scrolling and clipping content. Fixed: the header link row wraps, two-column blocks collapse to one, the tab strips scroll horizontally, and the legislation map's detail panel no longer extends the document.
  • SiteTabs are now linkable. Any tab on any page can be addressed directly — ets.html#price, cbam.html#calc, benchmarks.html#table — and the address bar follows the tab you are on, so a citation points at what you were reading. Previously only the benchmarks page did this, and the cross-link from benchmarks to the ETS price tab silently landed on the overview.
  • SiteDates. Every page now carries its own last-updated date instead of one site-wide "reviewed August 2026", and this changelog sits behind them.
  • SiteChart data is downloadable. Every chart now has a button underneath it that exports the plotted series as CSV, reflecting whichever series toggles are set. The legislation map exports all 107 acts with their number, title, year, policy area, status, EUR-Lex link and relations. These are the series as drawn, not the source datasets, and each page's caveats tab now says so.
  • AccessibilityCharts and tabs are now reachable without sight or a mouse. Every chart canvas carries a name and points at the CSV as its text alternative, rather than being an unlabelled blank to a screen reader. The tab strips implement the ARIA tab pattern — tablist, tab, tabpanel, aria-selected — and respond to left, right, home and end keys. Focus outlines are visible again. The legislation map, which is drawn on a canvas and cannot be made readable in place, carries a description pointing at the CSV and the search field.
  • HomeThe sourcing claim was overstated. The page said "every figure and every act links back to its official source". Acts do link individually; figures are sourced at dataset level, in each page's caveats tab. The claim now says which is which.
  • BenchmarksRemoved the opening sentence pointing out that benchmarks belong to the EU ETS — redundant now that the page sits inside the ETS section of the navigation.
  • RepoREADME, licence and setup notes added. The repository had no README and no licence, which left the "open resource" claim resting on nothing a visitor could check. The README sets out scope, sources, the two-level sourcing convention, the update policy and how to contribute; the licence is CC BY 4.0, with the EU's own texts and datasets carved out under their publishers' terms.
  • FixesA literal \u2014 escape was rendering as text in the CBAM scope tab. The opening sentence of the ETS page was ungrammatical. Four typos on the home page. The aviation page's "around €75" for the allowance price is now given as a range, since the price series on the ETS page ends in June 2026.
22 September 2026

The legislation map gets three levels of detail

  • Legislation map107 acts was too many to look at. The map now opens on core — the 37 acts that create a regime rather than adjust one, plus the live proposals that would materially change one. + machinery adds the delegated and implementing acts that operate each regime and the landmark revisions, for 78. All is the full 107 as before. The choice is remembered between visits.
  • Legislation mapThe level is set by hand for each act rather than computed from how connected it is. A connection count measures how often something has been amended, not how much it matters — filtering on it would have kept 23 of the 36 carbon pricing acts while dropping the Floods Directive, the Industrial Emissions Directive, ReFuelEU Aviation and the Methane Regulation. Every policy area keeps representation at core level.
  • Legislation mapOpening an act that sits below the level currently shown now raises the level automatically, so a link from the detail panel can never land on nothing.
  • Legislation mapTimeline nodes sharing a lane and a year are now stacked at fixed offsets instead of being scattered randomly, which was the main source of overlapping labels. The policy-area labels down the right-hand side were being clipped at common window widths and are now inside the fitted view, with a halo so a node in the final year cannot sit on top of one.
  • Legislation mapThe CSV export gained a column for the level, and the canvas now sizes itself to whatever the toolbar leaves rather than to a fixed height.

Cross-link corrections after the ETS 2 split

  • CorrectionEffort sharing pointed ETS 2 at the wrong page. "From 2028, ETS 2 will put a carbon price on fuels for buildings and road transport" linked to the ETS overview. It was written before ETS 2 had a page of its own; it now goes to that page.
  • EU ETSThe scope tab carried two signposts to the ETS 2 page, one of them a panel left behind when its chart moved. The panel is gone and its one load-bearing fact — 957 Mt reported for 2024, about 80% of what the original ETS covered — now sits in the "two systems" box alongside the single link.
  • EU ETSThe sources list still credited ETS 2 reported emissions to this page after the chart moved; it now says where the figure is quoted and where it is charted. The sentence pointing at the free allocation record was also missing a dash, which left it ungrammatical.
  • Effort sharing"Union Registry" linked to the ETS overview, which is not a page about the registry. The link now sits on "ETS allowances", which is what the ETS page is about.
  • BenchmarksThe link to the allowance price used the older #tab-price form. Normalised to #price, which is what the address bar shows when you open that tab.
