About this map

This is the companion to the climate legislation map. That map deliberately keeps to acts whose primary objective is climate change, which leaves most energy law outside it — even though the energy system is where most of the emissions reductions actually have to happen. This map picks up what it leaves out.

Scope. The map covers EU legislation adopted under the energy title of the Treaty (Article 194 TFEU) and the acts that operate it: renewables, energy efficiency and buildings, the electricity and gas markets and their networks, security of supply, fuels for transport, energy taxation, and — because it is part of the energy mix and increasingly of the argument — nuclear safety under the Euratom Treaty. Commission communications, action plans, strategies and recommendations are not legal acts and do not appear, however often they are cited: REPowerEU, the Electrification Action Plan and the Citizens Energy Package are described on the acts they led to, not drawn as nodes. The state aid guidelines that decide how renewables can be subsidised are left out on the same reasoning.

Renewables in focus. Renewable energy has its own lane, but a large share of the rules that decide how fast renewables are built sit elsewhere: contracts for difference in the electricity market reform, energy sharing in the electricity directive, rooftop solar duties in the buildings directive, offshore grid planning in TEN-E, renewable hydrogen quotas in the aviation and shipping fuel regulations. Highlight draws a ring around every act that carries renewables provisions, wherever it sits, and fades the rest; each such act says in its detail panel what those provisions are. Only hides everything else.

Overlap with the climate map. acts appear on both maps — the Renewable Energy Directives, the efficiency and buildings directives, the Governance Regulation, the gas package, the methane rules and the transport fuel regulations among them. They are marked “also on the climate map” in the detail panel. Carbon pricing, effort sharing and CBAM stay on the climate side.

Core, machinery, everything. The map holds acts. It opens with the core ( acts): the instruments that create a regime, plus the live proposals that would materially change one. + machinery adds delegated and implementing acts, landmark revisions and the 2022 emergency regulations. All adds the remaining : repealed predecessors and technical amendments. The line between the three is set by hand, act by act.

What is coming. Several announced proposals had not been tabled when this map was last updated and are therefore not on it yet: the revision of the energy security framework (gas, electricity and oil supply security), the post-2030 energy framework that would align the Governance Regulation and the renewables and efficiency directives with the 2040 climate target, a review of the hydrogen delegated acts, and measures on phasing out fossil fuel subsidies. They will be added as proposals once they exist.

How the list was compiled. The acts were checked against a EUR-Lex search for energy legislation (exported 26 September 2026, about 3,400 documents). Most of what such a search returns is deliberately left out: individual state aid and merger decisions, derogations and exemptions granted to single member states, recognitions of individual biofuel certification schemes, the hundreds of product-specific ecodesign and labelling regulations, trade defence measures, research programme decisions, corrigenda, and recommendations, which are not binding. Statuses follow the EUR-Lex in-force indicator; where EUR-Lex has consolidated an amendment, the act is shown as amended.

Getting the data out. “Download CSV” exports every act — number, title, year, policy area, status, tier, renewables relevance, whether it is also on the climate map, EUR-Lex link and relations — as a single file.
