EU climate policy, explained and visualised

EU climate policy is a complex system — directives, regulations, delegated acts and amendments layered over two decades, each written on the assumption that you already know the rest. It is genuinely hard to see through, and not only for newcomers: most people working on it know one corner well and the rest by reputation.

This site does two things. It explains how each instrument actually works, in plain language and from the legal text. And it visualises them, so the numbers a rule produces sit next to the rule itself. An orientation tool and a starting point for your own research, not a substitute for the legislation — every act links to its text on EUR-Lex, every chart names the dataset it was built from, and the plotted figures can be downloaded as CSV.

How to use this

Orientation, not legal advice

Everything here is compiled from the Official Journal, Commission publications and the EEA and Union Registry datasets. Sourcing works at two levels, and it is worth being precise about which: every legal act links to its own text on EUR-Lex, while figures are sourced at dataset level — each page's "data and caveats" tab names the release, the extract date and the field used, rather than footnoting each number individually. The visualisations necessarily simplify: they group, round and summarise. Where a judgement call was needed — how to classify an amending act, which acts fall inside the scope, how to read an ambiguous provision — the reasoning is set out on the page itself.

Every chart has a download button underneath it that gives you the plotted series as a CSV, reflecting whatever toggles you have set, and the legislation map exports all 107 acts with their relations. That is the series as drawn, not the source dataset — for the underlying data, go to the source named in the caveats tab.

Before relying on anything here for compliance, filings or contracts, check the consolidated text on EUR-Lex. Legislation moves quickly, and several of the acts shown are under active revision.

Dates, and what has changed

Because it moves quickly, every page carries its own last-updated date rather than a single site-wide one, and the changelog records what changed and when — including corrections, so that anything cited from here can be checked against what it says now. Tabs are addressable too: a link like ets.html#price or benchmarks.html#table opens that tab directly, and the address bar follows whichever tab you are reading.
